The Ruins of the Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu – A Window into Roman History in Oltenia
The ruins of the Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu, located in Gorj County, are a living testimony to the presence of the Roman Empire on the current territory of Romania. This castrum is part of a series of Roman fortifications built during the imperial rule, aimed at protecting and controlling trade routes and territories near the empire's borders. The discovery and subsequent research of these ruins have brought to light elements of great historical and archaeological value, offering us a detailed picture of military and civilian life from that period.
History and Context of the Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu
The Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu was built during the expansion period of the Roman Empire in Dacia, in the 2nd century AD, during the reign of Emperor Trajan. It was part of the system of Roman fortifications placed along the southwestern border of Dacia, to ensure the protection of the region and to monitor the movements of local tribes and potential enemies from the Balkans.

This castrum had the role of strengthening the Roman presence in Oltenia and protecting the adjacent areas from barbarian incursions. The ruins currently located near the town of Bumbești Jiu are remnants of a typical Roman fortification, including defensive walls, watchtowers, and a road system that connected with other Roman fortresses in the area.
Archaeological Discoveries
Archaeological research conducted in the area of the Roman castrum Bumbești Jiu has revealed a series of artifacts and structures that provide us with a clearer understanding of life from that period. Among the important discoveries are fragments of pottery, Roman coins, weapons, tools, and everyday objects, all indicating the military and civilian activities that took place within this castrum.

Additionally, the research has identified the remains of administrative buildings, castles, and housing for soldiers and civilians. It is possible that Roman infantry units were stationed here, given the strategic location of the castrum, as well as various commercial activities and supply provisions for the Roman garrison.
Architecture of the Castrum
The ruins of the Roman castrum Bumbești Jiu offer a clear image of the Roman construction style, with thick defensive walls and watchtowers at the corners. These fortifications were built from stone and brick, designed to withstand enemy attacks and provide effective long-term defense.

Another characteristic element is the drainage system and water pits that ensured the supply of drinking water, an important detail in the structure of a Roman fortification, which showed the Romans' concern for hygiene and the comfort of the soldiers.
The Importance of the Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu in the History of the Region
The ruins of the Roman Castrum Bumbești Jiu are important not only from the perspective of military history but also for understanding the process of Romanization in this region of Dacia. The placement of the castrum in a strategic area of Oltenia highlights the importance of the region for the Roman Empire, which sought to consolidate its control over Dacia after the wars with Decebalus.

In addition, the Roman castrum in Bumbești Jiu contributes to understanding the dynamics of the relationships between the Romans and local tribes, especially regarding trade exchanges and cultural influences. The objects discovered at the archaeological site reflect both Roman style and elements from the local culture, suggesting constant interaction between the two cultures.
